# Charging in 2026: what's changed

If your picture of public charging is a wallet full of cards, one app per network and a lot of slow posts, it is out of date. This page explains what is different now in Europe and the UK.

## Many more chargers

All big markets have grown fast. Countries count chargers in different ways, so don't compare the rows with each other. Compare each country with its own past.

| Country | Public charging points | As of | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Netherlands | about 210,000 (public, semi-public and fast), about 3.3 times the roughly 63,000 of 2020 | 2025 | [RVO via ANP](https://www.welingelichtekringen.nl/economie/laadpunten-elektrische-auto-s-verdrievoudigd-in-vijf-jaar) |
| Germany | 209,605, of which 54,341 fast (above 22 kW in the German definition) | 1 July 2026 | [Bundesnetzagentur](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/ladeinfrastruktur.html?nn=659906) |

| Belgium | more than 126,000 (public and semi-public) | first half of 2026 | [EV Belgium via The Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/news/2222534/belgium-adds-20000-ev-charging-points-in-six-months) |

Across the 27 EU countries there were 1,155,861 public charging points in mid-2026, a preliminary figure from the [European Alternative Fuels Observatory](https://alternative-fuels-observatory.ec.europa.eu/node/1536).

Public charging is mainly for longer trips and for people who can't charge where they park. Many owners do most of their charging at home or at work, where it is usually slower and cheaper.

## Paying: tap your bank card

You no longer need an account to charge.

- **EU, fast chargers.** Every new public charging point of 50 kW or more (built since 13 April 2024) must accept a bank card, through a card reader or a contactless reader. A QR code alone is not enough at these chargers ([Plugchoice on AFIR](https://plugchoice.com/en/news/afir-explained-data-and-payment-rules-for-public-charging)).
- **EU, slower chargers.** Below 50 kW, the operator may offer payment through a QR code and a web page instead. That also works without an account ([Plugchoice](https://plugchoice.com/en/news/afir-explained-data-and-payment-rules-for-public-charging)).
- **EU, older fast chargers.** From 1 January 2027, older public points of 50 kW or more on the main European road network (TEN-T) must take bank cards too ([Plugchoice](https://plugchoice.com/en/news/afir-explained-data-and-payment-rules-for-public-charging)).
- **Cash only is not allowed.** The European Commission says a charger where you can only pay in cash does not meet the rules ([EC questions and answers on AFIR](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/clean-transport/alternative-fuels-sustainable-mobility-europe/alternative-fuels-infrastructure/questions-and-answers-regulation-deployment-alternative-fuels-infrastructure-eu-20231804_en), Q5.7).
- **UK.** All public charge points of 50 kW or more must offer contactless payment, and so must new public points of 8 kW or more installed after 24 November 2024 ([GreenFleet](https://greenfleet.net/node/13288)). Operators must also let you pay through at least one roaming provider ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/regulations-public-charge-points)).

## Do you still want an app or a charging card?

It's optional. A roaming app or card works across many networks. It can be handy where a charger has no card reader, and it can sometimes be cheaper. A subscription is mainly worth it if you fast-charge often. Prices differ by payment method, so check before you choose: [Chargeprice](https://www.chargeprice.app/) compares the price of a session per card or app.

## The price is shown before you start

- **EU, 50 kW and more:** the price you pay without a contract is based on the price per kWh. The charger must show it before you start. A fee per minute for staying plugged in may come on top ([Plugchoice](https://plugchoice.com/en/news/afir-explained-data-and-payment-rules-for-public-charging); [EC Q&A](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/clean-transport/alternative-fuels-sustainable-mobility-europe/alternative-fuels-infrastructure/questions-and-answers-regulation-deployment-alternative-fuels-infrastructure-eu-20231804_en), Q5.17 and Q5.19).
- **EU, below 50 kW:** the price must be shown before you start too, but it doesn't have to be per kWh. It can be per minute or per session ([Plugchoice](https://plugchoice.com/en/news/afir-explained-data-and-payment-rules-for-public-charging)).
- **UK:** the total price must be shown clearly in pence per kWh ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/regulations-public-charge-points)).

Fast charging usually costs more per kWh than charging at home. Move the car when it is done, so you don't pay a fee for staying plugged in.

## Plugs

In Europe and the UK, cars sold new today use a **Type 2** plug for [[ac-charging|slower AC charging]] and a **CCS2** plug for [[dc-charging|DC fast charging]]. At a fast charger the cable is attached to the charger, so you don't need your own. Some older used cars, such as the first two generations of the Nissan Leaf, use the older CHAdeMO plug for fast charging instead ([our research note R-01](https://github.com/zkMenno/EV-Finder/blob/main/research/R-01-bidirectional-and-connectors.md)). More on our page about plugs and two-way charging.

## Speed: the charger's kW is a ceiling, not a promise

A "150 kW" charger can give up to 150 kW. How much your car takes depends on the car, the battery's charge level and its temperature. The car's [[peak-kw|top charging speed]] is reached only for a short time. A better guide is the [[10-80|charging time from 10 to 80 %]] or the [[dc-km-20min|range added in 20 minutes]]; our model pages show both. In winter, let the car [[preconditioning|warm up the battery]] on the way to the charger, or charging can be much slower.

## Motorways: a fast charger every 60 km on main routes

The EU rules (AFIR) set a minimum for the main European road network (the TEN-T "core" network). Since the end of 2025, there must be a charging site at least every 60 km with at least 400 kW in total and at least one point of 150 kW or more. By the end of 2027 this rises to 600 kW and at least two points of 150 kW or more ([Nationale Leitstelle Ladeinfrastruktur](https://nationale-leitstelle.de/en/bestand-ausbau/afir/)). In June 2026, 79 % of the core network met the 2025 rule, according to an analysis by Transport & Environment ([electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/07/20/only-one-eu-country-misses-afir-charging-target/)). So there are still gaps.

## Planning a trip

Many current electric cars plan charging stops in their own navigation and warm up the battery on the way. If you want more control, [A Better Routeplanner](https://abetterrouteplanner.com/) plans trips with charging stops for your car.

## Broken chargers

Chargers do break. In France, 89 % of public points were technically available in the latest 2026 count ([Avere-France via L'Automobiliste](https://lautomobiliste.fr/24/10/2026/recharges-electriques-automobilistes/); [Electra](https://www.go-electra.com/fr/newsroom/how-many-ev-charging-points-in-france-2026/)). In the UK, operators must keep their rapid chargers working 99 % of the time on average across their network, and publish the figure ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/regulations-public-charge-points)). Before you rely on one charger, check recent check-ins from other drivers in [PlugShare](https://www.plugshare.com/) or [Chargemap](https://chargemap.com/).

## Buying a wallbox from 2027

From **1 January 2027**, every newly installed or renovated charging point in the EU must support a standard called **ISO 15118-20**. This includes private ones, such as a wallbox at home or at work, for both AC and DC ([EUR-Lex, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/656](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2025/656/oj/eng); [go-e](https://go-e.com/en/magazine/afir); [KEBA](https://www.keba.com/en/news/emobility/afir-compliant-with-keba-kecontact-p40-and-p40-pro)).

What this means for you:

- **It is a language, not a feature.** ISO 15118-20 is the way the car and the charger talk to each other. It makes two-way charging possible on the software side. It does **not** mean the wallbox can send power back to your home or the grid. The rules don't make two-way charging or Plug & Charge compulsory ([go-e](https://go-e.com/en/magazine/afir); [KEBA](https://www.keba.com/en/news/emobility/afir-compliant-with-keba-kecontact-p40-and-p40-pro)).
- **Your current wallbox is fine.** An installed wallbox doesn't need to be replaced. The rule applies once it is renovated, for example when its technical settings are changed or it gets major repairs ([KEBA](https://www.keba.com/en/news/emobility/afir-compliant-with-keba-kecontact-p40-and-p40-pro)). There is no date after which a working home charger becomes illegal ([go-e](https://go-e.com/en/magazine/afir)).
- **Portable chargers are not affected.** Mode 2 chargers (the cable with a box that goes into a normal socket) don't have to meet this rule ([go-e](https://go-e.com/en/magazine/afir)).
- **Your car must speak it too.** The new language only helps if the car supports ISO 15118-20 as well. Makers rarely say whether their cars do, and many cars sold up to about 2025 use older versions ([R-01](https://github.com/zkMenno/EV-Finder/blob/main/research/R-01-bidirectional-and-connectors.md)). We show it per car where a source confirms it, and "not disclosed" otherwise.
- **Expect a slow start.** Testing and certification for ISO 15118-20 are still being set up, so support in practice may be patchy at first ([Codibly](https://codibly.com/blog/white-papers/iso-15118-grid-functions-charger)).
